Cell-Driven Design.
The methodology your CFO already follows.

CDD is a software development methodology in which the cell — not the function, not the class, not the service — is the fundamental unit of business logic. The cell is testable, observable, and addressable. Everything else is, frankly, an abstraction.

The argument

Software methodology has, for forty years, debated where the locus of correctness should live. Test-Driven Development put it in the test. Behavior-Driven Development put it in the scenario. Domain-Driven Design put it in the aggregate. We propose the next, natural step: put it in the cell.

The cell is the only unit of computation in business software that every stakeholder can read, write, and trust simultaneously.

In Cell-Driven Design, the cell is:

  • Addressable. Users!B14 is a permanent, stable identifier.
  • Observable. Its value is visible. Its formula is inspectable. Its history is in the version log.
  • Composable. Cells reference cells. The dependency graph is automatic.
  • Testable. A green cell with conditional formatting =A1=B1 is a passing assertion.
  • Auditable. Every edit has a name, a timestamp, and (sometimes) a comment.

The five principles of CDD

1. The cell is the test.

If a cell turns green, the test passed. If it turns red, the test failed. If it stays white, your conditional formatting is broken — but that, too, is information.

// Conventional TDD
test('user is active', () => {
  expect(user.status).toBe('active');
});

// CDD equivalent
conditional_format: if Users!E2 = "active" then green else red

2. The formula is the function.

Every cell that contains a formula is a pure function from its references to its value. The formula is the spec. The formula is the implementation. The formula is the documentation. Migrating between layers is a non-issue.

3. The reference is the API.

Other systems consume your data by referencing your cells. =IMPORTRANGE() is your public API. Versioning is achieved by adding a column.

4. The tab is the bounded context.

In CDD, the equivalent of a DDD aggregate is a tab. Users live on the Users tab. Orders live on the Orders tab. Joining them is a deliberate, named operation. The tab boundary protects your domain.

5. The link is the deployment.

To ship a feature in CDD, you change the cells. The change is live the moment you press Enter. Rollback is Ctrl-Z. CI/CD is the version history pane.

How CDD compares

Case study: a CRM rebuilt in CDD

In 2023, Tarpit AI replaced their commercial CRM with a CDD-native implementation. The new system consisted of 4 tabs, 18 named ranges, and one Slack channel called #sheet-watch.

Key metrics, six months in:

  • Time to ship a new field: 3 seconds (down from 2 weeks)
  • Time to onboard a new sales rep: 0 minutes (they already knew Sheets)
  • Bugs introduced by junior staff: up 400% (acknowledged)
  • Net Promoter Score among internal users: +62 (extremely high)
  • Net Promoter Score among the auditor: −98 (lowest possible)

Trade-offs were made. So is the case with every methodology.

Adopting CDD

We recommend the following sequence:

  1. Identify a low-stakes domain. (We suggest internal tooling, not payroll.)
  2. Open a new sheet. Title it precisely.
  3. Define your tabs as bounded contexts. Lock the schema (column headers) for at least one week.
  4. Write your business logic in formulas. Resist the urge to escape into Apps Script.
  5. Define conditional formatting rules before you populate the data. These are your tests.
  6. Share the link. The link is the deployment.

Frequently raised objections

"This doesn't scale." Correct. It is not designed to. See the migration journey.

"This has no type safety." Conditional formatting is the type system. A red cell is a type error.

"This is not auditable." Google Sheets keeps a full version history with named authors back to the creation of the document. We invite you to find a Postgres deployment that does this by default.

"This is not really a methodology." Neither was BDD until people wrote books about it.

Stop testing your code. Start formatting it.
